01Master lifecycle

Every request, whatever channel it arrives on, is normalised into a single ticket object within 60 seconds and then travels one path. The five lanes below map to the five specifications in the brief: intake, categorisation, routing, escalation, resolution.

Read it left to right. The only place a human decides where work goes is the Triage queue, and that queue exists solely to absorb what the classifier is not confident about — by design it should receive under 15% of volume once keyword and model tuning settles.

02Intake & normalisation

The brief's first requirement is "a standardised entry point for omni-channel submissions." The critical design decision is that channels are not the entry point — the normaliser is. Each channel gets a thin adapter; everything downstream sees one shape.

This matters because the organisation cannot realistically force everyone onto a portal. People will keep WhatsApping their manager. So instead of banning channels, each one is instrumented and funnelled into the same object. The portal is the preferred channel because it captures structured fields cheaply, not the only one.

Design choice: urgency is never asked as a single free question. The form asks who is affected (just me / my team / my whole site / the company) and what is blocked (nothing / some work / all my work / revenue or safety). Priority is computed from the pair. This removes the universal problem of every request arriving marked Urgent.

03Categorisation taxonomy

Five top-level departments, each with a small closed set of request types. The taxonomy is deliberately shallow — two levels — because deep trees are where classification accuracy and human patience both die.

ITAccess & accounts · Hardware · Software & licences · Network · Security incidentpassword, login, VPN, laptop, printer, wifi, install, access, locked out, phishing, mail not workingIT Service Desk
PayrollSalary discrepancy · Payslip · Tax & declarations · Reimbursement · Bank detailssalary, payslip, PF, tax, TDS, deduction, reimbursement, claim, not credited, bonus, arrearsPayroll / Finance
OperationsFacilities · Procurement & assets · Travel · Vendor · Safetychair, AC, housekeeping, stationery, ID card, cab, booking, purchase, vendor, unsafeAdmin & Facilities
OtherUnclassified · Cross-functional · Suggestion— (no confident match)Triage desk

How classification actually decides

  1. Explicit override. If the requester picked a category in the portal, it is used as-is with confidence 1.0. Humans get the benefit of the doubt.
  2. Weighted keyword scoring. Each department carries a scored lexicon; subject-line hits weigh double body hits. Fast, transparent, auditable — and this alone handles the majority of traffic.
  3. LLM intent classification runs in parallel on free-text channels, returning a category, a confidence and a one-line reason. Where both agree, confidence is boosted.
  4. Confidence bands. ≥ 0.75 auto-route silently · 0.45 – 0.75 auto-route but flag for the group lead to confirm · < 0.45 hold in triage for a human.
  5. Every reclassification is training data. When an agent moves a ticket to another department, the original text, the wrong prediction and the correct label are written to a review set the lexicon and prompt are tuned against monthly.

Why not pure AI classification: a keyword layer that a non-technical HR lead can read and edit is worth more in year one than three points of accuracy. When Payroll adds a new claim type, they add words to a list — no retraining, no engineer, no deploy. The model handles what the list cannot.

Assignment rules, in precedence order

#RuleBehaviour
1ContinuityA reopened ticket returns to the agent who resolved it, if they are available. Context beats load balance.
2SpecialisationCertain types are hard-pinned — security incidents to the security handler, bank-detail changes to a named payroll officer. No round-robin.
3Site & languagePrefer an agent on the requester's site or shift and able to reply in their language; fall back to the central group.
4Weighted loadLowest sum of open-ticket weights, so one P1 is not treated as equal to one P4.
5AvailabilityAgents on leave or off-shift are excluded via the same HRIS calendar that drives SLA. No ticket sits with someone on holiday.
6FallbackIf the group is empty or capped, the ticket goes to the group's backlog with the lead as accountable owner — never to nobody.

The accountability rule: a ticket always has exactly one accountable human. When it cannot be an agent, it is the group lead. This single constraint is what closes the "ambiguous ownership" gap named in the brief.

05Priority matrix & SLA

Priority is computed, not requested. Impact and urgency are collected as plain-language answers and resolved through a fixed matrix.

Impact × urgency → priority

Impact ↓ / Urgency →Blocked entirelyMost work blockedSome work affectedNothing blocked
Company / site-wideP1P1P2P3
Whole teamP1P2P2P3
A few peopleP2P2P3P4
Only meP2P3P3P4

Overrides applied after the matrix

  • Safety, harassment or security keywords force a minimum of P1 regardless of what the requester selected.
  • Statutory deadlines (tax declaration windows, statutory leave notice, full-and-final settlement) force P2 and set a hard calendar due date that overrides the rolling SLA.
  • Payroll cut-off proximity — any salary-affecting request raises one level inside the three working days before payroll processing.
  • Repeat contact — a third follow-up on the same ticket raises priority one level and notifies the group lead. Chasing is a signal, not noise.

Service level targets (business hours, requester's site calendar)

PriorityAcknowledgeFirst human responseResolveUpdate cadence
P1 CriticalInstant, automated15 min4 hHourly
P2 HighInstant, automated2 h1 business dayDaily
P3 NormalInstant, automated8 h3 business daysEvery 2 days
P4 LowInstant, automated1 business day5 business daysOn change

The clock runs on business hours and pauses in exactly two states: Awaiting Requester and Awaiting Approval. It does not pause for Awaiting Third Party — a vendor's delay is still the organisation's delay, and hiding it behind a paused clock is how SLA reporting becomes fiction.

Trigger type A
Time-based

The four rungs above, driven purely by percentage of SLA consumed. Fully automatic.

Trigger type B
Severity-based

P1 skips L0 entirely and pages the on-call handler the moment it is created, including out of hours.

Trigger type C
Volume-based

Five or more similar tickets in 30 minutes declares a major incident, creates a parent ticket, and broadcasts one update to all linked requesters.

Trigger type D
Sentiment & repetition

Third follow-up, or negative sentiment detected on a reply, raises priority a level and pings the lead — before the complaint reaches HR informally.

Escalation is not reassignment. The ladder adds watchers and accountability; it only takes the ticket away at L2, and only if the assignee has not touched it for two hours after breach. Punishing agents by yanking work at the first nudge produces silent SLA gaming, not faster resolution.

Transition rules that are enforced, not conventional

  • A ticket cannot reach Resolved without a resolution code and a resolution note. This is what makes reporting possible at all.
  • Finalized is immutable. A reopen inside 14 days reactivates the same ID with a reopen counter; after 14 days a new ticket is created and linked. Reopen rate then becomes a real quality metric.
  • Every transition writes an append-only audit entry: who, when, from, to, why. Nothing in the ticket's history can be edited or deleted.
  • Auto-close on requester silence always sends two warnings first (day 3 and day 5) and can be undone by a single reply.

08Resolution pathway

Closure is a verified event, not an agent's opinion. Three things must be true before a ticket finalises: the work is done, it is coded, and the requester has been told on the channel they used.

Step 1
Resolution recorded

Agent writes the outcome and picks a resolution code — Fixed, Configuration change, Policy answer, Not eligible, Duplicate, No response, Cancelled.

Step 2
Requester notified

Reply lands on the original channel. WhatsApp requests get a WhatsApp answer; nobody is forced into a portal to read their outcome.

Step 3
Confirmation window

72 hours to dispute with one tap. Silence finalises. A dispute reopens the same ID and returns it to the same agent.

Step 4
Feedback captured

One question — was this resolved? — plus optional comment. Scores roll up per group, never per individual agent, to avoid gaming.

Step 5
Knowledge harvested

Repeated resolution patterns are drafted into knowledge-base articles, which then power deflection at intake.

Step 6
Archived

Ticket locked, retained per policy (payroll and grievance records held longer), and available read-only for audit.

10Failure modes of the system itself

A workflow diagram that only shows the pipeline working is a marketing diagram. These are the ways the pipeline breaks and what happens then.

Channel adapter down (WhatsApp API outage)Heartbeat per adapter; no messages for 15 min during business hours raises an alertAuto-reply on that channel names the portal and the helpline; queued messages replay on recovery, timestamped at original receipt so SLA is not penalised to the requester's cost.

Nobody adopts the system; WhatsApp-to-manager persistsChannel mix report; a manager forwarding on someone's behalf is flaggedManagers get a one-tap "log this for my report" action rather than being told off. Adoption is designed for, not mandated.
